# Origins of Retrieval-Augmented Generation

The term Retrieval-Augmented Generation was introduced in a 2020 paper by Patrick Lewis and colleagues at Facebook AI Research, working with University College London and New York University. The paper combined a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence generator with a dense retriever over Wikipedia.

The authors described the generator as parametric memory and the retrieved Wikipedia passages as non-parametric memory. Grounding answers in retrieved passages helps a language model give up-to-date and traceable answers, and it reduces hallucination compared with relying on model weights alone.

Standard RAG retrieves passages by similarity to the question, so it struggles when the answer depends on facts spread across several documents.
